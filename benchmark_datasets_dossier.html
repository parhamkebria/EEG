Dataset survey · EEG classification · August 2026

Where EEGIMAGE goes after Berkeley

Sixteen publicly available EEG datasets that recent papers actually benchmark on — chosen so that your feature-image + CNN framework can be compared against published numbers rather than re-implemented baselines.

Current benchmark: Berkeley Synchronized Brainwave · 1 channel · ~30 subjects Prepared for: journal submission

The framing problem

What counts as "the same task"

The Berkeley set is a single-channel consumer recording labelled by stimulus condition — blink, math, music, video, colour, think-of-an-object. That paradigm has essentially no modern successor with a competitive literature, which is why there is nobody to compare against. No reviewer will accept "state of the art" on it.

EEGIMAGE, though, is not tied to that paradigm. It renders raw values, statistics, band powers and spectra into 2D arrays and classifies them with a CNN — a generic trial-to-label encoder. That means the right move is not to find one Berkeley replacement, but to enter three arenas that each carry their own published leaderboard.

Arena A — where the numbers are

The 12-dataset suite used by EEG foundation-model papers (LaBraM, BIOT, CBraMod). Same splits, same metrics, ready-made comparison table against EEGNet and EEG-Conformer.

Arena B — where the protocol is

MOABB motor-imagery datasets. A maintained Python benchmark with fixed within/cross-session evaluation and a public ranking of ~30 pipelines.

Arena C — where the name fits

Visual perception and mental-imagery EEG. Directly justifies the "IMAGINATION / EEGIMAGE" framing, and it is the fastest-moving corner of the field.

Arena A

The foundation-model benchmark suite

This is the highest-value target. CBraMod (ICLR 2025) and LaBraM (ICLR 2024) both evaluate on the same twelve datasets with the same protocol and the same three metrics — balanced accuracy, Cohen's κ, weighted F1. Reproduce that protocol and your results table writes itself: EEGNet, EEG-Conformer, BIOT, LaBraM and CBraMod are already in the column beside you, and the κ / weighted-F1 pair is exactly the right answer to the class-imbalance problem your repo already flags.

Arena B

MOABB, for a protocol you don't have to defend

MOABB (Mother of All BCI Benchmarks) is a maintained Python library wrapping 160+ datasets behind one interface, with fixed within-session, cross-session and cross-subject evaluations and a published ranking of standard pipelines (CSP+LDA, FBCSP, Riemannian tangent space, EEGNet, ShallowConvNet). Plugging EEGIMAGE in as a pipeline object gives you a comparison that a reviewer cannot accuse of favourable splitting — the splits aren't yours.

Four datasets carry the paradigm well: BNCI2014-001 (BCI Competition IV-2a; 9 subjects, 22 channels, 4 classes, 2 sessions — the field's default sanity check), Cho2017 (52 subjects, 64 channels), Lee2019-MI (54 subjects, 62 channels, 2 sessions, 11,000 trials) and Dreyer2023 (87 subjects, 27 channels, ~240 trials each, plus personality and cognitive profiles — mean online accuracy 63.4% ± 17.4, with over half the cohort at chance).

Dreyer2023 is the interesting one for your subject-shift problem: it ships user profile data, so "which subjects does the model fail on, and why" becomes an analysable question rather than a limitation paragraph.

Cheap win

MOABB's Lee2019 and Cho2017 loaders download and epoch the data for you. Getting all four datasets running is roughly a day of work, and it produces the single most defensible table in the paper.

Arena C

Visual perception and imagery — the name-fit datasets

If the framework is called IMAGINATION / EEGIMAGE, a reviewer will look for imagery data. This corner also has the newest releases and the loudest 2024–2026 literature, which is where citations come from.

THINGS-EEG2 (Gifford 2022)

Perception · zero-shot
10 subj · 63 ch · 1000 Hz · train 1654×10×4 · test 200×1×80

The default benchmark for EEG visual decoding. Evaluation is 200-way zero-shot retrieval, so it tests representation quality rather than closed-set classification — a genuinely different claim from your current one.

Do not use uncritically — block-design leakage

The 2017 Spampinato "EEG-ImageNet" 40-class dataset (the one behind many 90%+ accuracy claims) presented all images of a class in one contiguous block. Li et al., The Perils and Pitfalls of Block Design for EEG Classification Experiments (TPAMI 2021), showed those accuracies collapse to near chance under randomised presentation, and Ahmed & Wilbur (CVPR 2021) replicated the finding. Any paper that cites those numbers as a baseline in 2026 will be desk-checked.

The same caution applies in weaker form to EEG-ImageNet (2024): images are grouped by category and the official split takes the first 30 images of each category for training and the last 20 for test, so trials adjacent in time land on opposite sides of the split. If you use it, report a temporally-blocked split alongside the official one and say so explicitly.

Also worth knowing

Two datasets that are about to matter

HBN-EEG — the Healthy Brain Network release used for the NeurIPS 2025 EEG Foundation Challenge: over 3,000 participants across 11 releases, six tasks (resting state, surround suppression, movie watching, contrast change detection, sequence learning, symbol search), downsampled to 100 Hz, fully BIDS, with response time, demographics and psychopathology factor scores as targets. It is the largest open, task-labelled EEG corpus available, and cross-subject generalisation is the explicit challenge. Data: s3://nmdatasets/NeurIPS2025/ and the challenge data page.

ERP CORE — 40 participants, six optimised paradigms yielding seven ERP components (N170, MMN, N2pc, N400, P3, LRP, ERN), with full scripts and processing pipelines. Not a classification benchmark, but the standard reference for showing that a representation preserves known neural components. A single figure using it buys a lot of neuroscientific credibility. Available via erpinfo.org/erp-core.

Recommendation

A four-phase evaluation plan

Ordered by return per unit of effort. Phases 1 and 2 alone are enough for a solid journal submission; 3 and 4 are what make the paper feel like it belongs to the imagery literature.

Phase 1

Adopt the foundation-model protocol

Rebuild the pipeline against the LaBraM / CBraMod preprocessing and report balanced accuracy, Cohen's κ and weighted F1. This is the single change that converts your work from "an approach" into "an approach with a comparison table."

FACED · PhysioNet-MI · TUEV · TUAB
Phase 2

Wrap EEGIMAGE as a MOABB pipeline

Run the library's standard within-session and cross-subject evaluations. Splits, preprocessing and statistics come from the library, so the comparison against CSP+LDA, FBCSP, Riemannian and EEGNet is unarguable.

BNCI2014-001 · Cho2017 · Lee2019-MI · Dreyer2023
Phase 3

Earn the name

One perception benchmark and one true imagery benchmark. The imagery dataset has published EEGNet baselines and two sessions per subject, so it supports both the cross-subject and cross-session claims in one table.

Visual Imagery BCI (Sci Data) · THINGS-EEG2
Phase 4

Close the loop on Berkeley

Keep the original result as a low-channel ablation rather than a headline: show accuracy as a function of channel count from 64 down to 1, using MindBigData's device families. That turns your dataset's weakness into a stated contribution.

MindBigData 2022 · Berkeley Synchronized Brainwave
Methodological checklist for the paper

Keep the subject-aware split you already have, but state it as leave-one-subject-out or a fixed subject-disjoint split, and report per-subject variance — cross-subject means hide enormous spread (Dreyer2023: 40% to 99% on a binary task).

Fit every normalisation, channel-wise scaling and feature-image colour mapping on training subjects only. A per-trial rendering step that touches global statistics is a leakage path reviewers now look for.

Report against baselines you did not tune yourself wherever the dataset has published numbers, and re-run at least one baseline (EEGNet is the conventional choice) inside your own pipeline to show the harness is fair.
